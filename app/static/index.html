    Instructions and rules for your current career.

    Career guide

    Develop your roster, simulate games, then complete the postseason, draft and required roster cuts before starting the next season.

    You were hired before Week 17. New careers inherit a 3–13 record from the first 16 games; your GM record starts with the final regular-season game. Season 1 is introductory, followed by four full contract seasons (Seasons 2–5).

    Rebuild challenge: build a contender through the draft and player development.

    Team ratings

    Ratings and development

    Current is ability today; potential is a possible ceiling. Both are out of 99. Growth and decline follow position-specific age curves. Kickers have seasonal form changes.

    After training camp, players with expired contracts can be re-signed beside their roster entry. Review and confirm the offered annual pay and term, based on ability and position: franchise players (90+) get 5 years; low-rated players (below 70) 2; other veterans (28+) 4; other young players 3. Re-sign or release expired players before continuing. Guaranteed-career re-signings must fit the cap immediately. Older careers may temporarily exceed it before final cuts. New veteran offers use position and ability, with premium pay for stars, scaled to the signing season’s cap. Existing active deals stay fixed; inherited contracts keep their original pay.

    New veteran demands use a smooth accelerating curve: players rated 60 or below on the salary-demand average ask for the minimum, while prices rise increasingly quickly toward each position’s elite salary at 99. The signing-season cap scales the curve, subject to the season’s minimum pay. Signed contracts, inherited deals and rookie draft-slot salaries are unchanged.

    New veteran salary demands use (current rating + potential rating) ÷ 2, including half points, with the player's position and signing-season cap. Camp development sets the ratings before an offer is calculated. Signed salaries, inherited deals and rookie draft-slot pay stay fixed. Contract lengths still use current rating and age.

    Drafted rookies remain provisional during the draft and training camp. Their expected draft-slot salary is reserved against the upcoming cap, but they have no signed deal yet. Only rookies retained when you continue to the next season sign their full contracts; released rookies leave no contract or cap charge. Existing signed deals stay fixed. Unsigned rookie holds do not satisfy the payroll floor.

    After the draft, start training camp to apply offseason development and rookie assessments once. Review the updated roster, then make final cuts before continuing into the new season. Reloads and retries preserve the same camp results.

    Defense and line stats are aggregate estimates. Tackles include sacks; pass snaps include sacks.

    How starters work

    The highest current-rated players start automatically at every position. Backups rotate according to position and ability.

    Roster limits

    Every team starts with 35 players. Regular-season roster: 31–38 players. Offseason draft picks may temporarily increase the roster. Position minimums appear above each group.

    Player salaries are annual amounts, fixed for the signed term. Starting pay uses position and current ability; rookie pay uses the actual draft selection. Roster replacements earn the minimum salary for one year ($1M in Season 1). Contract years begin in the player's first playing season. Players with expired contracts keep their last salary and roster place while you make offseason decisions; current careers must re-sign or release them before the next season. For cap-enabled careers, payroll starts with a $200M cap, enforced when finalizing the post-draft roster. Drafting may temporarily exceed the cap. The payroll floor starts at $160M; falling below it is a warning until free agency exists. All retained salaries count, including renewal-due players; under older rules, releases clear the full annual amount without penalties. Fully guaranteed careers keep released signed salaries on the cap through the original contract end; retirement clears remaining salary and unsigned camp rookies leave no dead cap. Open Payroll breakdown in Roster to review signed salaries, provisional rookie holds, expired-contract holds and active dead-cap charges by player. These add up to the displayed cap usage; the floor remains a warning only. Review the inline release preview for annual dead-cap charges, total remaining obligation, cap space after the cut and any required replacement cost. While over cap, cuts that leave vacancies add weak minimum-salary replacements to maintain roster and position minimums. Older saved seasons remain playable; cap enforcement begins with a cap-enabled offseason. Annual increases depend on the saved career’s salary rules. New careers inherit payroll between that season’s floor and cap, including a few costly multiyear deals for weaker players.

    Each season, the cap grows 5%, rounded to the nearest $1M from the previous rounded cap ($200M → $210M → $221M). The payroll floor is 80% of that cap, rounded to the nearest $1M. New rookie pay and the minimum salary grow 5%, rounded to $0.01M each year ($1.00M → $1.05M → $1.10M → $1.16M). Signed salaries never inflate, even when below the new minimum. Draft payroll uses the upcoming playing season’s cap and pay scale. Older unfinished drafts keep their original rules.

    Draft rules

    Six rounds · Same order each round · CPU teams balance player quality and position needs. Rookies join your roster without replacing anyone. The regular-season roster range is 31–38.

    Keep your earlier picks and let the CPU finish the draft using player quality and position needs. Review roster cuts afterward.

    How to make cuts

    Use Drop beside a player in Ratings, then confirm. Keep players by leaving them on the roster. Total roster and position minimums are protected.

    Schedule rules

    8 group 5 same-place 4 rotating

    Playoff format

    Reseeded after every round: highest remaining seed plays lowest. Higher seeds host; the championship is at a neutral site.

    Scroll sideways to follow all four rounds. Future matchups appear after reseeding.

    Tiebreakers

    How records are counted

    Career totals include the current season. Playoff appearances count when the final field is set; byes do not count as wins.

    Your tenure began in Season 1, Week 17. The first 16 inherited games count toward team standings and player stats, but not your GM W–L–T.

    Contract rules

    Make the playoffs at least once in your first four seasons or lose the job, even if winning records earn extensions. Each season earns only the highest reward: championship +5 years; otherwise playoffs +2; otherwise a winning record (more wins than losses) +1; otherwise no extension. Extensions add to your remaining contract. After the opening deadline, an expired contract ends your career.
